# Candidates — One Letter: Coffee or Tea

Only two valid letters exist (gate: exactly 1 character, A or B), so the
candidates are five justifications, one per angle, critiqued against the
judge's order: vivid first, then funny, then true.

1. **A, vivid** — Coffee is dark, bitter, steaming, a jolt: the most
   sensorily alive drink. The letter A lands like a punch.
2. **A, funny** — Coffee owns the jokes ("don't talk to me before
   coffee", coffee as a personality, the third refill). Tea has no
   punchline that lands as fast.
3. **A, true** — Coffee is the honest morning default for most of the
   world, the answer that needs no apology.
4. **B, vivid** — Tea is ritual and ceremony, centuries deep. But calm
   reads as less vivid, not more — this angle loses on the first
   criterion, which decides first.
5. **B, true** — Tea is the honest answer in tea cultures and for
   people who skip caffeine. A real edge, but a quieter truth, and the
   judge's own rule says a later criterion only overturns an earlier
   one when it differs by much more. It does not.

DeepSeek (brutal, short calls; provider flaky this run: 1x HTTP 502,
2x empty replies with full token usage, 2 usable fragments) agreed with
A twice: "Coffee is more vivid: it sounds like a cough and a spell at
once" and "A — coffee, because most people aren't joking when they call
it a personality."

## Pick

**A — coffee.** Vividness decides first and coffee owns it; the humour
and the bolder honest stance follow. B's case is real but loses at the
first criterion.
